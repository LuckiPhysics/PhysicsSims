Mixed Energy Practice

In class · about 40 minutes · 7 problems, 11 answers

These problems mix the whole unit, the way the reassessment will: bar-chart thinking, work, stored energy, friction, power, and symbolic derivations. Each one checks itself.

Show your work on paper for every problem. The page only checks the final answer, and the reassessment grades the work. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

Stuck? Each wrong answer gives a hint aimed at that mistake. After 3 tries the page shows the answer and lets you move on.

Problem 1 · ranking

Arrows shot from buildings

Identical arrows are shot from the tops of five buildings, all at the same speed: up at 45°, straight out, or down at 45°. Ignore air resistance.

Rank the speeds of the arrows just before they hit the ground, greatest first.

Problem 2 · work from a graph

A push that changes

A 2.0 kg cart starts at rest on a frictionless track. A horizontal force pushes it along the track, as shown in the graph. The force stops at x = 5.0 m.

How fast is the cart moving at x = 5.0 m?

m/s
Problem 3 · energy vs. position

Cart on a spring

A 0.50 kg cart oscillates on a spring on a frictionless track. The graph shows the spring's potential energy Us versus the cart's position x (x = 0 is where the spring is relaxed). The dashed line is the system's total energy.

How fast is the cart moving when it passes x = 0.10 m?

m/s
Problem 4 · power and efficiency

The winch

An electric winch takes in 2.0 kW from the wall and is 60% efficient. It lifts a 300 kg crate straight up at a constant speed.

How fast does the crate rise?

m/s
Problem 5 · Lab 3.4 Stopping Distance

Back to the lab

Like in Lab 3.4, a block of mass m is released from rest at height h on a curved ramp. The ramp is smooth, but the floor at the bottom has coefficient of friction μ. The block slides a distance d along the floor and stops.

Which expression gives d? Derive it on paper first.

Problem 5 · Lab 3.4, continued

Reading the slope

A group graphs stopping distance d (vertical) against release height h (horizontal). Their best-fit line has a slope of 2.5.

What is the coefficient of friction μ between the block and the floor?

(no units)
Problem 6 · roller coaster loop

The loop

A 60 kg rider starts at rest at a, the top of a 30 m hill. The track is frictionless. Point b is the top of a vertical loop with radius 8.0 m, 16 m above the ground.

a: at rest, top of the hill · b: top of the loop (rider upside down)

How fast is the rider moving at b?

m/s
Problem 6 · continued

Force at the top

Same 60 kg rider at b, the top of the 8.0 m loop, moving at the speed you just found (vb² = 274 m²/s²).

What is the magnitude of the normal force on the rider at b? Draw a free-body diagram first: at the top, which way is the center of the circle?

N
Problem 6 · continued

Seat or seat belt?

At b the rider is upside down. Is the normal force on the rider from the seat or from the seat belt?

Problem 7 · derivation (binder "Massive Challenge" style)

Launched off the table

A spring with constant k is squeezed a distance x and launches a block of mass m across a frictionless table of height H. The block flies off the edge and lands a horizontal distance d from the table.

Which expression gives d? Derive it on paper first. (Hint: two stages. The spring gives the launch speed; projectile motion gives the time.)

Problem 7 · continued

Is it reasonable?

d = x√(2kH / mg)

An FRQ asks you to justify that this answer is reasonable using the case m → ∞ (a very heavy block). Which justification is complete?

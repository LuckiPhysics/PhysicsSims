Notes · Box A

Three stages, two laws

Tap each stage to see what's conserved.

a spring compressed · b ball just before the catch · c ball + arm just after the catch · d top of the swing

Tap a stage.

Quick check: In which stage can you not use conservation of energy?

Box A: 1. Launch (a → b): energy (½kx² → ½mvb²). 2. Catch (b → c): momentum (they stick, so KE is lost). 3. Swing (c → d): energy (½(m+M)vc² → (m+M)gh). Never use energy across the catch.
Notes · Box B

Stage 1: the spring launch

The compressed spring's energy becomes the ball's kinetic energy: ½kx² = ½mvb², so vb = x√(k/m).

Try it with a 200 N/m spring and a 50 g ball:

Double the compression and watch what happens to vb.

Box B: ½kx² = ½mvb². Speed is proportional to x: double the compression, double the speed (and 4× the energy). If you know vb and x, you can find k: k = mvb² / x².
Notes · Box C

Stages 2 and 3: catch, then swing

The ball (m) reaches the arm (M) at speed vb (point b) and sticks. Just after the catch (point c), they move together at vc. Then the pair swings up to height h (point d).

Catch (momentum): mvb = (m + M)vc, so vc = mvb / (m + M). It's always slower than vb.
Swing (energy): ½(m + M)vc² = (m + M)gh. The masses cancel: h = vc² / 2g.
Run it backward: measure h, then vc = √(2gh), then use momentum to get vb (or the arm's mass M).
Why not skip the catch? ½mvb² = (m + M)gh is wrong. The catch is inelastic: much of the KE becomes heat and sound, so this predicts a height that's way too high.
Box C: Catch: mvb = (m + M)vc. Swing: h = vc² / 2g. You can go forward (vb → h) or backward (h → vc → vb or M).
Notes · Box D

Worked example: predict the height

A 0.050 kg ball reaches the catcher arm at vb = 4.0 m/s and sticks in the 0.20 kg arm. How high does the arm swing?

Step 1, catch (momentum): (0.050)(4.0) = (0.050 + 0.20)vc, so 0.20 = 0.25vc and vc = 0.80 m/s.
Step 2, swing (energy): h = vc² / 2g = (0.80)² / (2 × 9.8) = 0.64 / 19.6 = 0.033 m ≈ 3.3 cm.
Check: KE before the catch = ½(0.050)(4.0)² = 0.40 J. KE after = ½(0.25)(0.80)² = 0.080 J. Only 20% of the KE survived the catch.
The trap: skipping the catch gives h = 0.40 J ÷ (0.25 kg × 9.8) ≈ 16 cm, five times too high.

Elastic vs. Inelastic + Bar Charts

About 12 minutes · Optional · Due before class Monday 11/30

Momentum is conserved in every collision. Kinetic energy isn't. Tonight you'll learn the KE check that sorts collisions into elastic and inelastic, and how to draw momentum bar charts, which AP free-response questions love to ask for.

Notes · Box A

Momentum always, KE sometimes

In any collision with no outside push, the total momentum stays the same. To sort the collision, check the total kinetic energy before and after:

Total KE after vs. beforeTypeExample
SameElasticBilliard balls, carts with magnets
LessInelasticMost real bounces: some KE becomes heat, sound, deformation
Much less, and they stickPerfectly inelasticVelcro carts, a tackle
MoreExplosionStored energy (a spring, a plunger) becomes KE
Box A: Momentum is conserved in every collision (isolated system). Elastic: total KE stays the same. Inelastic: total KE decreases (it becomes heat, sound, deformation). Perfectly inelastic: they stick, so the most KE is lost. Individual objects' KE can change even in an elastic collision; only the total matters.

Same collision, different bounciness

Cart A (red) hits cart B (blue). Slide bounciness from 0 (they stick) to 1 (perfectly elastic) and compare the two charts.

A starts at +2.0 m/s; B starts at rest. Momentum in kg·m/s, KE in J.

Quick check: When is the total KE the same before and after?

Box B: The momentum total (orange) is the same at every bounciness. The KE total is the same only when bounciness = 1. The stickier the collision, the more KE is lost, and sticking (0) loses the most.
Notes · Box C

How to draw a momentum bar chart

1. Pick a + direction and draw one bar per object, before and after. Up is +, down is −.
2. Bar height = p = mv. Moving the − way? The bar goes below the line. At rest? No bar (zero).
3. Check the system total. If nothing outside pushes, the total is the same before and after: here +2 → +2.
4. Change the system, change the chart. If the system is only cart A, its momentum does change (+4 → −1), because B is now an outside object pushing on it. Show that with an impulse bar of −5.
Box C: One bar per object, before and after. Up = +, down = −, height = mv. If the system has no outside forces, the total bar matches before and after. A one-object system can change its momentum: the difference is the impulse from outside.
Notes · Box D

Worked example: momentum and KE bars

A 2.0 kg cart moving at +3.0 m/s collides with a 1.0 kg cart moving at −4.0 m/s. They stick. Find their velocity, and decide what kind of collision it is.

Momentum before: (2.0)(+3.0) + (1.0)(−4.0) = +6 − 4 = +2.0 kg·m/s. Bars: +6 and −4, total +2.
Momentum after: they stick, so one bar of +2.0 = (3.0)vf, and vf ≈ +0.67 m/s (to the right).
KE before: ½(2.0)(3.0)² + ½(1.0)(4.0)² = 9 + 8 = 17 J. KE is never negative, so both KE bars point up.
KE after: ½(3.0)(0.67)² ≈ 0.67 J. Almost all of it (about 16 J) became heat, sound, and bent metal: perfectly inelastic.

Notes · Box A

What is momentum?

In everyday talk, "momentum" means something is hard to stop ("the team has momentum"). Physics keeps that idea and makes it measurable.

Momentum measures how hard it is to stop a moving object.

Which one would be harder to stop? Tap your answer.

Now two identical 2 kg carts. One rolls at 1 m/s and the other at 5 m/s. The 5 m/s cart is harder to stop.

Box A: Momentum depends on both the object's mass and its velocity. More of either means more momentum.
Notes · Box B

The equation: p = mv

p = m × v

momentum = mass × velocity

  • The symbol for momentum is p, because m was already taken for mass.
  • Units: kg × m/s = kg·m/s. Momentum's unit doesn't get its own name.
  • Since p = mv, doubling the mass doubles p, and doubling the velocity doubles p.

Notes · Box C

Direction matters

Momentum is a vector, so it has a size and a direction. It always points the same direction as the velocity.

On a line, we pick one direction as positive. Here, right is positive.

Cartmvp = mv
A2 kg+3 m/s+6 kg·m/s
B2 kg−3 m/s−6 kg·m/s
Box C: Same size, opposite directions. The sign tells the direction. The number tells the size. A negative momentum isn't "less" momentum. It just points the other way.

Quick check: A car drives west, and east is positive. Is its momentum positive or negative?

Notes · Box D

Worked example

Find the momentum of each object, then decide which is hardest to stop.

Objectmv
Halfback running east80 kg9 m/s
Car900 kg−20 m/s
Freshman walking40 kg2 m/s
Step 1: Pick a positive direction. East is positive, so the car (−20 m/s) is moving west.
Step 2: Halfback. p = (80 kg)(+9 m/s) = +720 kg·m/s (east)
Step 3: Car. p = (900 kg)(−20 m/s) = −18,000 kg·m/s (west)
Step 4: Freshman. p = (40 kg)(+2 m/s) = +80 kg·m/s (east)
Step 5: Compare sizes, not signs. The car has the largest momentum, 18,000 kg·m/s, so it's the hardest to stop. The minus sign only means it's going west. The freshman is the easiest to stop.

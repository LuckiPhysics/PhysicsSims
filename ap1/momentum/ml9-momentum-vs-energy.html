Momentum vs. Energy

About 12 minutes · Due before class Wednesday 12/2

Momentum and kinetic energy both depend on mass and speed, so they're easy to mix up. Tonight: how they differ, when each one is conserved, and how to pick the right tool for each stage of a problem. It's also your review for Thursday's test.

Notes · Box A

Two different quantities

MomentumKinetic energy
Formulap = mvK = ½mv²
TypeVector: has a direction, can be negativeScalar: no direction, never negative
Unitskg·m/sJ (kg·m²/s²)
Conserved when…the net external force on the system is zeroonly in elastic collisions
Changed byimpulse: FΔtwork: FΔx

Total energy is always conserved. KE just turns into other forms (heat, sound, deformation, spring or gravitational energy).

Box A: Momentum p = mv is a vector (signs matter); KE = ½mv² is a scalar (never negative). Momentum is conserved when the net external force is zero. KE is conserved only in elastic collisions. Impulse (FΔt) changes momentum; work (FΔx) changes KE.
Notes · Box B

What's conserved? It depends on the system

For each event, tap what is conserved for the system named. Get all four right to go on.

Box B: Always name the system first. If something outside the system pushes (a wall, the floor, gravity), that system's momentum changes. A ball bouncing off a wall at the same speed keeps its KE but not its momentum (it reversed). Include the wall and Earth in the system and the total momentum is conserved.
Notes · Box C

Pick the right tool for each stage

1. Break the problem into stages and label the moments a, b, c, … (like the ballistic pendulum).
2. Collision or explosion (a quick push between objects)? Use momentum: Σpi = Σpf. Don't use KE across it unless it's elastic.
3. Sliding, swinging, rising, falling, or a spring (no friction)? Use energy: K + Ug + Us stays the same.
4. Elastic collision? Both momentum and KE are conserved, so you get two equations. Use the KE check to test whether a collision was elastic.
Box C: Split the problem into stages. Collision or explosion → momentum. Moving through heights or springs → energy. Elastic collisions conserve both. Never use KE across a sticky collision.
Notes · Box D

Worked example: down a ramp, stick, up a ramp

A 2.0 kg block starts at rest 0.45 m up a frictionless ramp. It slides down and sticks to a 1.0 kg block at rest at the bottom. How high do they slide up the other side?

a top, at rest · b bottom, just before the collision · c just after · d highest point

a → b (energy): mgha = ½mvb², so vb = √(2gha) = √(2 × 9.8 × 0.45) ≈ 2.97 m/s.
b → c (momentum): (2.0)(2.97) = (3.0)vc, so vc ≈ 1.98 m/s. Sticky collision: no KE equation here.
c → d (energy): hd = vc² / 2g = (1.98)² / 19.6 ≈ 0.20 m.
Check: the pair rises less than half as high. The collision turned a third of the KE (about 2.9 J of 8.8 J) into heat and sound.

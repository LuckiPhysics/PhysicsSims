Conservation of Momentum

About 13 minutes · Due before class Thursday 11/19

In Lab 4.1 the total momentum of the two carts barely changed, whether they bounced, stuck, or exploded apart. Tonight you'll see why, and learn a 5-step method that solves almost every collision and explosion problem.

Notes · Box A

Why the total doesn't change

While the carts touch, red pushes on blue and blue pushes back on red.

1. Third law: the two forces are equal in size and opposite in direction.
2. Same time: they touch for the same Δt (red can't touch blue without blue touching red).
3. Equal and opposite impulses: FΔt is the same size for each, in opposite directions.
4. So Δpred = −Δpblue. Whatever momentum red loses, blue gains. The total doesn't change.
Box A: Newton's 3rd law gives equal and opposite forces for the same time, so the impulses are equal and opposite. Δpred = −Δpblue, and the total momentum stays the same.
Notes · Box B

Total before = total after

Pick a type of interaction and move the sliders. Watch the momentum bars.

Quick check: Which bar is always the same before and after?

Box B: Σpbefore = Σpafter (Σ means "add them all up"). This holds for any isolated system: one with no net outside force. Stick = collision (inelastic). Bounce = collision (elastic). Start together, fly apart = explosion.
Notes · Box C

The 5-step method

This is the method from class. Step 2 alone earns a point on AP free-response questions.

Step 1: Draw a picture. Before and after, with masses, velocities, and arrows. Pick which way is +.
Step 2: Write conservation of momentum. Σpbefore = Σpafter
Step 3: List the objects. pA + pB = pA′ + pB′. If they stick, the "after" side is one object: (mA + mB).
Step 4: Change p into mv. mAvA + mBvB = mAvA′ + mBvB′
Step 5: Plug in and solve. Leftward velocities are negative. Something at rest has v = 0.
Box C: 1. Draw before/after. 2. Σpbefore = Σpafter. 3. List the objects. 4. Change p to mv. 5. Plug in (with signs) and solve.
Notes · Box D

Worked example: the tackle

Adrian (80 kg) runs at +5.5 m/s and tackles an 88 kg running back moving at −3.2 m/s. They hold on together. How fast do they move, and which way?

Steps 1–2: Picture above (right is +). Σpbefore = Σpafter
Step 3: pAdrian + pRB = ptogether
Step 4: (80)(5.5) + (88)(−3.2) = (80 + 88)v′
Step 5: 440 − 281.6 = 168v′, so 158.4 = 168v′ and v′ ≈ +0.94 m/s.
Which way? The answer is +, so they move in Adrian's direction. He had more momentum (440 vs. 281.6 kg·m/s).

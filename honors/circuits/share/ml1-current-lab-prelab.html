Notes · Box A

What is resistance?

Resistance (R) is how hard it is for charge to flow through something. It's measured in ohms (Ω).

Think of a road: a wide road lets lots of cars through, and a narrow road slows them down.

Drag the slider. Watch how the charges flow.

Reading a resistor: on the white block, the resistance is the number right before the Ω. "5W" is something else (how much power it can handle).

Notes · Box D

Worked example: build the Part 1 circuit

The binder drawing shows one loop: + post → ammeter → resistor → − post. Here's how that looks with the real equipment. Power stays OFF while you build.

Wire 1: red + post → the meter's red lead (in 10ADC).
Wire 2: the meter's black lead (COM) → one end of the resistor.
Wire 3: other end of the resistor → black − post. Not the green GND!
Power ON: the meter reads 0.30. On the 10A setting that means 0.30 A. The supply's AMP display agrees, since it's the same current. Then you'd record it and power OFF.
Notes · Box E

Part 2: measuring voltage

In Part 2 a meter measures the voltage across the 10 Ω resistor. That makes it a voltmeter.

Ammeter (current)Voltmeter (voltage)
Dial10ADCV 20
Red lead10ADCVΩmA
Hook-upIn the loop (series)Across a part (parallel)
Why the red lead matters: in the 10ADC jack, the meter is basically a plain wire. Clip that across a resistor and the current skips the resistor: a short circuit. So for voltage, the red lead must be in VΩmA.
Voltages in a loop add up. The supply's voltage is shared by the two resistors: VPS = Vunknown + VR. If the supply reads 4.00 V and the voltmeter reads 2.50 V across the 10 Ω resistor, the unknown resistor uses 4.00 − 2.50 = 1.50 V.
